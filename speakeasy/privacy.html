What stays on your phone

  • Translation. Text is translated with Google ML Kit's on-device translation, using language packs stored on your phone. The text itself is not sent anywhere to be translated.
  • Reading text in photos, the live camera and documents. Text is recognised on your phone (Google ML Kit; on iPhone also Apple's Vision; for a few languages such as Arabic, Russian and Tamil on Android, an open-source reader called Tesseract).
  • Live camera. Camera frames are read in memory and dropped right away. They are never saved or sent anywhere. A picture is kept only if you choose to save it.
  • Photos and documents. The app works on temporary copies and deletes them afterwards. By default it does not keep captured photos or original documents; you can change that with Keep captured photos and Keep original documents in Settings.
  • Summaries and questions about documents. These are worked out on your phone, by the app itself or, where your phone has one, by its built-in on-device AI (Google's Gemini Nano on supported Android phones, Apple's on-device model on supported iPhones). Your document text is not sent to a server for this.
  • History and saved phrases. Your translations and phrases are stored in the app on your phone. You can turn off Save history, delete items, set Delete history automatically, or use Clear all data.
  • Voice recordings. SpeakEasy never stores recordings of your voice. Only the text that was recognised is kept, and only in your history.
  • Home screen widgets. Widgets show your languages and saved phrases. They never show your history or documents.
  • Sharing into SpeakEasy and PDF export. Text, images and PDFs you share into the app, and PDFs you export, are handled on your phone.

When something leaves your phone

Speech recognition for "Needs internet" languages

For some languages your phone cannot recognise speech on the device. SpeakEasy then shows "Needs internet" and, when you tap the microphone, your phone's speech service receives the audio to turn it into text: Google's speech service on Android, Apple's on iPhone. Their own privacy terms apply: Google Privacy Policy, Apple Privacy Policy. Where a language is marked "On-device", speech is recognised on your phone.

Reading aloud with an "Online voice"

SpeakEasy reads translations aloud with voices installed on your phone. If a language only has a voice that needs the internet, the app labels it "Online voice" and uses it only when you are online; the text being read is then processed by your phone's speech engine provider (on Android usually Google). You can install an offline voice from the app to avoid this.

Downloads: language packs and text-scanning data

  • Translation language packs are downloaded from Google by ML Kit. Under Google's ML Kit terms, ML Kit may also send Google technical information about how the feature performs, such as errors and usage counts; it does not send the text you translate. See ML Kit Terms.
  • Text-scanning data for some languages (for example Arabic, Russian and Tamil on Android) is downloaded from GitHub (raw.githubusercontent.com), only when you choose to download it. Like any web server, GitHub sees your IP address for the download. No content from the app is sent.

Advertising (Google AdMob)

SpeakEasy is free and shows ads from Google AdMob, only on some screens and never while you are recording, talking, scanning or reading. To show ads, measure them and prevent fraud, Google may collect:

  • your device's advertising ID (on Android) or, only if you allow tracking, the advertising identifier on iPhone;
  • your IP address, used for a general location such as the country;
  • device and app information, such as the model, operating system, language and app version;
  • information about the ads shown and tapped.

SpeakEasy never gives Google, or anyone else, the text, speech, photos or documents you translate.

  • Consent. In the European Economic Area, the UK and Switzerland, and in US states with privacy laws, SpeakEasy asks for your choices with Google's consent form before personalised ads are used. You can change them at any time in Settings → Ad privacy choices (shown where it applies).
  • iPhone tracking. On iPhone, SpeakEasy asks for permission to track only after a short explanation, and only if ads are shown. If you say no, you still see ads, just not personalised ones.
  • Android advertising ID. You can reset or delete it in your phone's settings (Settings → Privacy → Ads, or Google → Ads).

How Google uses this information: How Google uses information from sites or apps that use its services.

"Remove ads" purchase

Where it is offered, you can remove ads with a one-time purchase. Payment is handled entirely by Google Play or the App Store; we never see your payment details or account. SpeakEasy keeps only a note on your phone that you are ad-free, and when that was last checked with the store. For buyers, the ads SDK is not started at all. All features work the same with or without the purchase. Refunds are handled by Google or Apple.

What we don't do

  • We don't sell or rent your data, and we don't share your content with anyone.
  • We don't ask you to create an account or sign in.
  • We don't upload what you type, say, photograph or open to our own servers. We have none for SpeakEasy.
  • We don't lock translation features behind ads or payments.

Permissions

SpeakEasy asks for each permission only when you first use the feature that needs it, with an explanation first. Every feature has a way to continue if you say no.

  • Microphone: for voice and conversation translation, only while you are speaking.
  • Speech recognition (iPhone): to turn your speech into text.
  • Camera: for photo and live camera translation.
  • Tracking (iPhone): only for ads, as described above.
  • Location (Android): declared for a place-lookup feature that is not available in this version. SpeakEasy does not ask for or use your location.

To choose photos and documents, SpeakEasy uses your phone's own picker, so it sees only the files you pick. It does not ask for access to all your photos or files.

Children

SpeakEasy is not directed to children under 13 and we do not knowingly collect personal information from children. If you believe a child has used the app in a way that concerns you, contact us and we will help.

Your rights and choices

Depending on where you live, you have rights over your personal information, for example under the EU General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR), the UK GDPR, the California Consumer Privacy Act (CCPA/CPRA) and India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023. They can include the right to access, correct or delete your data, to object to or restrict processing, to withdraw consent, and to complain to a data protection authority.

Because we do not receive your content and have no account for you, most of your data is only on your phone and fully under your control:

  • Delete your data: use Settings → Clear all data, delete individual history items, or uninstall the app.
  • Ads: change your choices in Settings → Ad privacy choices, in your phone's ad settings, or at Google My Ad Center. For data Google holds, see the Google Privacy Policy.
  • Purchases: your purchase history is held by Google Play or the App Store.
  • Anything else: email npmtechsolutions@gmail.com. We will answer within 30 days. We don't discriminate against anyone for using these rights.

Where the GDPR applies, the legal basis for ads is your consent (or our legitimate interest in funding a free app, for non-personalised ads), and for the app's features it is providing the service you ask for.

Security

Your history and settings stay in the app's private storage on your phone. The ad-free note is kept in your phone's secure storage. Downloads are checked before use, and the app connects only over HTTPS. No method is perfect, so please also keep your phone protected with a screen lock.

Changes to this policy

If we add a feature that changes what leaves your phone, we will update this policy before it is switched on, change the effective date above, and, where the change is important, tell you in the app.
